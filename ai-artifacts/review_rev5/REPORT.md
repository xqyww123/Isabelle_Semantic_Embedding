### lock-connection-lifecycle — dismissed

## Spec-relaxation proposals

### Relax D12's "there is no limit on how many judge sessions run at once" to a modest concurrency cap (a semaphore around the judge session only, not around gate start)
Decision affected: D12 (§2, plan:63) — "One entity per judge session ...; the session is started the moment the entity's answer arrives; there is no limit on how many judge sessions run at once"

Gain: It collapses three of this review's findings into one line and removes the reasoning burden from several more. Unbounded concurrency is what turns the fixed 2 s `RateLimitError` arm into a lockstep storm (up to _BATCH_SIZE = 20 gates from one `answer` call, times the theories `schedule_dag` runs at `Multithreading.max_threads ()`), and a 429 is precisely the signal that too many sessions are talking to the API at once — so the cap attacks the cause rather than adding jitter to the symptom. It also bounds the number of gates that can be sitting inside a failing `embed` call at once, and makes "the prefilter is disabled once per run" trivially true instead of a test-and-set straddling a long await. Cost per §5.5 is measured (8-20 s, $0.06-0.13 per judge), so a cap also bounds the money one runaway theory can spend in a burst. Implementation is an `asyncio.Semaphore` acquired inside `_gate` around step 3 only: gates still START the instant an answer arrives, so D12's ordering property and the queue's termination argument are untouched.

Cost: Wall clock on a large first-interpretation batch: with N judges at 8-20 s each, a cap of k serialises them into ceil(N/k) rounds instead of one. The interpretation session stays open through them (§5.3 termination), so a theory's total time rises. The cap value becomes a new constant nobody has measured, and picking it wrong (too low) is a real slowdown on exactly the workload the feature is meant to make cheaper. D12's wording would change, and the plan would gain a knob it currently, deliberately, does not have.

### Relax §15.8 item 7's "the task spans ALL of the theory's entries" to "the task spans all entries but only enrolled entries are addressable and counted" (a derivation of D13, not D13 itself)
Decision affected: Not a §2 decision — §15.8 item 7 (plan:1052-1061), a derivation the plan makes from D13; raised here because it is the single root of two separate findings and the owner will see its consequences in §12

Gain: The widening is what removes today's "Unknown entry" guard (semantic_interpretation.py:592-593) and what rebases the four user-visible counters, because `task.results` is simultaneously the addressing table, the progress denominator and the completeness certificate. Making enrolment — not membership — the thing that governs addressability and counting fixes `on-answer-not-enrolled` and `user-visible-counters-rebased` at one place instead of two, and restores the property that the run cannot be made to gate, judge or mint an entity it never asked about. It also keeps `working_names` (semantic_interpretation.py:1191) meaning "what you must interpret" rather than "every entity of this theory", so the query tool stops refusing lookups the agent can do today.

Cost: The three roles of `task.results` have to be separated explicitly: `state` becomes the certificate and the counter source, and `results` becomes storage only. That is a small amount of extra structure in `AgentTask`, and it means two places (the answer tool's address filter and the completeness check) now consult `state` rather than one dict. The widening itself must stay — a dependent that is not a seed still needs an index, a state and a result slot — so this buys clarity, not fewer entries.

## Nitpicks punished

Four surviving items were nitpicks or overreach, and I want them counted as such rather than quietly folded into the ruling list.

1. `put-interpretation-shape` (dismissed) is the clearest. Its load-bearing claim — that adding an invalidating put to `Gate_Writer` makes the raw-put grant decorative and orphans two live tests — is simply false: the grant exists only for writers that skip invalidation (semantics.py:998-1015), `put_interpretation` invalidates unconditionally, and both cited tests exercise `update_gate_fields` alone and stay green. What is left after that is a class docstring sentence that will be rewritten while the method is added, dressed up as a design finding. Its proposed `_put_fields` that infers invalidate-vs-check from the field names is actively worse than what it replaces.

2. `row1-discards-stored-version` (dismissed) was filed as "major" for a record shape that two independent scans of a 1.44M-record production store showed occurs zero times, and that no writer in the repository can produce, because digest and version land in the same put. It reasoned entirely from a defensive `else` branch (semantic_interpretation.py:949-951) whose live input set is empty, and asked for that dead branch to be preserved into the new design.

3. `lock-connection-lifecycle` (dismissed), half (b). Trading §8.2's stated, one-glance-checkable invariant ("the connection is never released to the pool: closing it is what releases the lock") for one avoided loopback connect on a path that only fires when another run genuinely holds the lock is the wrong side of this repo's own rule about invariants that cannot be violated versus invariants that must be remembered. Half (a) at least identified a real ordering question; it was answered by counting scheduling hops, not by adding a release procedure §8.1 deliberately excluded.

4. Overreach inside items I otherwise upheld, which I have discounted in the rulings rather than rewarded: `write-rec-none-deref`'s "three of the four branches want the stored version" (false — one does, and its proposed restructure would erase a proven guard); `tracked-two-predicates`'s corruption scenario (a reductio of a reading the snippet's own comment and three live sub-tests rule out, filed as a blocker); `judgetask-bends-the-queue-machinery`'s claim that the blanket `except Exception` is "precisely the failure D10 says must never be hidden" (D10 prescribes exactly that outcome, and §12 approves the channel); `judge-throttle-unbounded`'s "retries forever" (bounded, and its proposed fix would spend more quota against an account that just ran out); and `degradation-is-invisible`'s sub-claim (c), which asks for a new user-visible line and concedes it needs approval — i.e. a §12 re-opening filed as a defect.

The pattern worth naming for the next round: several concerns found a genuine one-line slip in §15's pseudocode and then inflated it into a design defect plus a restructure. The slips are real and worth fixing; the restructures were, in every case where I checked, worse than what they replaced.
